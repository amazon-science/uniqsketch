# TreeUniqSketch (v2.0) accuracy validation

**Status:** Validation record for the v2.0 release (tree index + hierarchical
query). Benchmarked on simulated binary mixtures. The first part (September 2026)
records the study that set the 2.0 tree defaults; the sections after it are the
earlier reproduction check against the original TreeUniqSketch, kept for the
record and annotated where later work changed the interpretation.

## 2.0 tree defaults: what the read-origin oracle showed

The simulated reads carry their source genome in the read id
(`@AWS_Ec711_0_0/1`), so every signature hit can be classified as **true** (the
read came from the reference it hit) or **noise** (it came from another genome and
matched through sequencing errors). Everything below uses that split; the earlier
sweeps counted both as evidence.

### Setting

Same 8 phages and 280 mixtures as the sections below. The hard case is a
close-family member (one of Ec112/Ec377/Ec688/Ec711) at 0.01 %: 28 mixtures.
0.01 % of 3 M read pairs is ~300 reads, 0.27× coverage, and a close member's
distinguishing sequence is ~0.6 % of its genome (151–447 unique 81-mers in 2–3
loci). Per-base error rate of the reads, measured by 81-mer survival against the
source genome: 0.26 % (R1) / 0.41 % (R2). The dominant strain sits at ~2700×.

### Information ceiling

Taking each contaminant's *entire* unique-k-mer pool (no selection, no
thresholds), only **31 contaminant reads across all 28 mixtures** carry an intact
signature k-mer. 17/28 mixtures have ≥ 1 such read; **4/28 have ≥ 3**. With
`--rcutoff=2` (more than 2 reads) no signature scheme can exceed 4/28. Under the
margin-2 pool the ceiling is 25 reads, 16/28 mixtures with ≥ 1, 3/28 with ≥ 3.

### Noise

A margin-1 signature one substitution from an abundant sibling's k-mer is hit by
every sibling read with a sequencing error at that base, and one such read hits
up to 71 consecutive signatures, exactly like a true read. Over the 28 mixtures:

| index | true reads on contaminant | noise reads on contaminant | reads on absent references | strain calls at `r>2, h≥10` (TP / FP) | at `r≥1, h≥10` (TP / FP) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `-c50`, margin 1 (previous default) | 18 | 23 | 71 | 8 / 4 | 10 / 4 |
| `-c500`, margin 1 | 31 | 60 | 2093 | 12 / 42 | 18 / 44 |
| `-c500 --min-margin=2 --strict-margin` | 25 | 1 | 2 | 3 / 0 | **16 / 0** |
| `-c50 --min-margin=2 --strict-margin` | 25 | 1 | 1 | 3 / 0 | 10 / 0 |

Of the 8 previous-default detections, 7 had a true read but were carried over the
threshold by noise, 1 was pure noise. Margin-2 strict removes the noise (a false
hit needs two coincident errors) while keeping 25 of 31 true reads; the close
members retain 62–99 % of their pools (Ec688 447 → 279 is the worst). With noise
gone, a single true read yields 24–71 hits (it covers a divergent locus) and the
three residual noise reads yield 1–5, so a single-read rule recovers every mixture
that has evidence. `-c50` reaches fewer because its sparser signatures give a read
fewer hits; `-c500` takes each close member's whole pool.

### Resulting 2.0 defaults

1. `uniqsketch --tree` selects with `--min-margin=2 --strict-margin` (opt out with
   `--min-margin=1`); parameters recorded in `<outdir>/build_params.tsv`.
2. `querysketch --tree` reads that record and defaults `--rcutoff` to 0 on a
   strict-margin index (explicit `--rcutoff` wins; older indexes keep 2).
3. Abundance is hits per signature (`count / sigs_total`), renormalised over the
   reported rows. With `-c500` the references carry 119–500 signatures and raw
   counts misreport a 10 % contaminant as anything from 2.6 % to 32 %; hits per
   signature gives 9.3–11.3 %. Equal signature counts make the two identical.

### Full sweep with the 2.0 defaults (`--tree=10000,1000,100 -c500 -k81`, 280 mixtures)

| contaminant % | strain-level detections / 56 | of which close members / 28 | family-level (unresolved close member) | main detected | false strain calls |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 0.01 | 44 | 16 | 7 | 56 | 1 |
| 0.1 | 56 | 28 | 0 | 56 | 0 |
| 1.0 | 56 | 28 | 0 | 56 | 1 |
| 3.0 | 56 | 28 | 0 | 56 | 0 |
| 10.0 | 56 | 28 | 0 | 56 | 0 |

At 0.01 % every distant contaminant is resolved (28/28; `-c50` resolved 7). The two
false calls are single reads carrying two errors: `count` 10 and 20, where genuine
single-read calls carried 24–71; both below 0.1 % abundance. Abundance error of the
contaminant (mean absolute, percentage points): 0.006 at 0.01 %, 0.020 at 0.1 %,
0.078 at 1 %, 0.108 at 3 %, 0.183 at 10 % (maximum 1.29). Family rows for
unresolved 0.01 % members read 0.009–0.012 %.

For comparison, the previous default (`-c50`, margin 1, `r>2`) on the same 280
mixtures: 19 strain-level detections at 0.01 % (10 close), 75 false calls across
the sweep (strain and family rows, all ≤ 0.08 % abundance, a dozen per abundance
level), abundance MAE 0.021–0.211.

### Margin-2 cost on a bacterial set

120 complete *E. coli* RefSeq genomes, k=81, `-c1000`: every reference kept at
least 244 strict-margin signatures (mean 75 % of the margin-1 count); the closest
pair (pool 1036) kept 570. No reference fell below 50.

### Within-group leaf signatures (September 2026)

**Why.** A leaf's global pool (k-mers in no other genome of the index) is small
for a strain with close relatives, but mostly not because of the relatives: on the
120 *E. coli* genomes, the k-mers of a strain absent from its siblings but present
in some unrelated genome outnumbered its globally unique k-mers by a median 3.2×,
and by 26× for ASM4073563v1 (global pool 3,295 k-mers; within its 2-member group,
86,475). Those k-mers separate the strain from its siblings whenever their carriers
are absent from the sample, which for any given sample is almost all of them.

**What is built.** For every internal node with direct leaf children, candidates
unique among the node's descendant leaves are selected for its direct leaves with
the same margin and filters as ordinary signatures, and each signature's carriers
(other genomes of the index containing it) are recorded in
`<outdir>/sketch_group.tsv`. Signatures already in the leaf sketch are not
repeated. At query time a within-group signature is scored only if none of its
carriers is a candidate leaf after the coarse levels; global signatures are always
scored. Build: `uniqsketch --tree` default, `--no-group-signatures` to opt out
(recorded in `build_params.tsv`); query: `--no-group-signatures` ignores the file.

**Index.** E. coli (`--tree=1000000,300000,100000,10000 -c5000 -k81`): 27 groups,
20.95 M within-group candidates, 226,458 selected of which 26,307 were already
global signatures; 200,151 written over 76 leaves, 143,947 with at least one
carrier. Benchmark strains, global + within-group: ASM4073563v1 1255 + 2083,
ASM130812v1 592 + 245, ASM5700863v1 1253 + 429, ASM2155987v1 3018 + 2490,
ASM2052682v2 1363 + 323. Phage index: 23 signatures added.

**280-mixture sweep (E. coli), same scoring as above.**

| index | strain-level at 0.01 % / 56 | close members / 28 | family-level | false rows (whole sweep) | contaminant MAE, pp (0.01 → 10 %) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|---|
| 2.0 defaults, global signatures only | 35 | 8 | 13 | 0 | 0.007 → 0.358 |
| + within-group signatures | 39 | 11 | 11 | 0 | 0.006 → 0.317 |

Everything from 0.1 % up is 56/56 on both. Carrier masking withheld signatures in
117 of the 280 mixtures (up to 2,180 in one) and no false row appeared. Phage sweep:
the detection table is identical to the 2.0 defaults (counts differ in 44 mixtures
where the extra signatures collect extra hits).

**Sibling-pair benchmark (36 mixtures).** Pairs from the same group at 0.01, 0.03
and 0.1 % (`ecoli_ref/wg_pairs.tsv`, reads in `simulated/wg_reads/`), queried with
and without the within-group set, scored with the read-origin oracle:

| | resolved to strain / 36 | family-level | spurious rows | noise reads on the contaminant |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| global only | 18 | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| within-group | 19 | 4 | 0 | 0 |

The count barely moves because at 0.01–0.03 % the limit is reads, not
signatures. The evidence behind the calls moves a lot where the pool was the
limit: ASM4073563v1 as contaminant at 0.1 % went from 1, 4 and 1 supporting
reads to 28, 51 and 53 in its three mixtures (the third from a family-level to a
strain-level call), and at 0.03 % from 1 to 10. Where the global pool was already
adequate (ASM98787v1, 2,690 signatures) nothing changes. Carrier masking withheld
up to 864 signatures in the 16 mixtures with a carrier candidate.

**Cost.** Build: the group build runs as flat parallel job lists (see
`GroupSketchUtil.hpp`); at 32 threads the E. coli tree build is 169 s with the
set and 136 s without (the sequential first implementation took 1,379 s; the
O(1) substitution hashing in `isTwoSafe` took the pair from 306/257 s to
169/136 s with byte-identical indexes). Disk:
3.4 GB of per-group candidate files against 22 GB the index writes anyway. Query:
25.6 s vs 25.3 s on one 3 M-pair mixture at 32 threads, warm cache (an earlier
578 s vs 305 s sweep comparison was a cold-cache artefact; the "on" sweep ran
first on freshly simulated reads).

**Artifacts.** `simulated/ecoli_ref/`: `run_wg_pipeline.sh` (the whole
comparison), `wg_pairs.tsv`, `run_wg_simulation.sh`, `wg_report.py` →
`wg_report.txt`, `wg_sweep_on/`, `wg_sweep_off/`, `idx_wg_c5000/`
(`idx_wg_c5000_seq/` is the sequential build, byte-identical), `pool_group.tsv` /
`pool_flat.tsv` (pool sizes), `rebuild_wg_par*.sh` (parallel build checks and the

## TL;DR (July 2026 reproduction check)

- The v2.0 **tree** query reproduces the original TreeUniqSketch abundance
  estimates almost exactly: **Pearson r = 0.9989 (R² = 0.998)** on log10 detected
  abundance across 224 jointly-detected contaminants; mean absolute difference
  **0.08 percentage points**.
- Where the two differ, v2.0 is **mildly better** — slightly more sensitive at the
  detection floor and slightly more accurate on abundance — with a negligible
  increase in tiny (< 0.1 %) false calls. No regression.
- Main reference detected in **100 %** of samples at every abundance; contaminant
  detected in **100 %** at ≥ 1 %, ~98 % at 0.1 %; 0.01 % is at the coverage floor.

## Benchmark setup

**References.** 8 phage genomes (~170 kb each), `simulated/all_set/AWS_Ec{112,151,
226,253,377,647,688,711}.fasta`.

**Samples.** 280 paired-end mixtures, 3 M read pairs each
(`simulated/reads/reads_3000000_abund_<main>_<contam>_<pct>_R{1,2}.fastq`):
8 mains × 7 contaminants × 5 mixing ratios (contaminant at 0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 3.0,
10.0 %). Ground truth is the mixing ratio in the filename — expected main =
`100 − pct`, expected contaminant = `pct`.

**Index (public v2.0 binary).**

```bash
uniqsketch --tree=10000,1000,100 -c50 -t32 -k81 \
    -o tree/sketch_uniq.tsv -f tree/outdir -r tree/db_uniq_count.tsv @all_set.txt
```

The tree groups the 4 near-identical genomes {Ec112, Ec377, Ec688, Ec711} into one
cluster (internal node, representative Ec377) and keeps the 4 distinct genomes
{Ec151, Ec226, Ec253, Ec647} separate — 8 leaves, 1 internal node, level-1 sketch
of 5 representatives, full-resolution leaf sketch of all 8.

**Query.**

```bash
querysketch --sensitive -t32 --tree=tree/outdir --ref tree/sketch_uniq.tsv \
    --r1 R1.fastq --r2 R2.fastq --out out.tsv
```

The full sweep (all 280 × tree, plus flat for context) is `scripts/run_all_queries_v2.sh`.

## Tree results

Detection is scored per sample against the filename ratio; abundance error is the
mean absolute difference (percentage points) over detected contaminants.

**Contaminant (56 samples per abundance level):**

| expected % | detected / 56 | detection rate | mean abs err (pp) |
|-----------:|--------------:|---------------:|------------------:|
| 0.01 | 15 | 27 % | 0.018 |
| 0.1  | 55 | 98 % | 0.023 |
| 1.0  | 56 | 100 % | 0.079 |
| 3.0  | 56 | 100 % | 0.111 |
| 10.0 | 56 | 100 % | 0.214 |

**Main reference:** detected in all 280 samples (100 % at every level); mean
absolute abundance error ranges 0.015 pp (at 0.01 % contaminant) to 0.21 pp (at
10 %).

**False positives** (a detected reference that is neither main nor contaminant):
0.15 rows per sample, maximum magnitude 0.08 %. Effectively clean.

The 0.01 % row is the coverage floor: a 0.01 % contaminant is ~300 read pairs over
a 170 kb genome (~0.26× coverage), so most signature k-mers are simply not
sequenced. Detection at ≥ 0.1 % is essentially complete.

## Reproduction check: v2.0 tree vs. original TreeUniqSketch

The original results (`simulated/results_tree/summary.tsv`, produced by the old
main-repo binary querying the same c=50 tree leaf sketch) are the closest
apples-to-apples reference for the v2.0 tree path. Comparing contaminant detected
abundance over the same 280 samples:

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| Pearson r (log10 detected abundance, n = 224) | **0.9989** |
| R² | 0.998 |
| mean \|v2.0 − old\| detected abundance | 0.084 pp |
| detection agreement (of 280) | 264 (224 both-detect, 40 both-miss) |
| detections v2.0 gained (old missed) | 14 |
| detections v2.0 lost (old detected) | 2 |

**Which is better — v2.0 tree.** The two are near-identical (r = 0.9989), and where
they diverge v2.0 is slightly ahead:

- **Sensitivity:** v2.0 detects the contaminant in more samples at the floor
  (0.01 %: 15/56 vs 5/56; 0.1 %: 55/56 vs 53/56); net +12 detections.
- **Accuracy:** lower mean abs error at essentially every level, for both the
  contaminant and the main (e.g. main at 10 %: 0.21 vs 0.35 pp).
- **Cost:** v2.0 emits a few more sub-0.1 % false calls (0.15 vs 0.02 per sample) —
  the expected flip side of higher sensitivity — but no large spurious calls in
  either (max FP < 0.09 % for both).

So v2.0 reproduces the reference and nudges it forward: a small net improvement in
sensitivity and abundance accuracy, not a regression.

## Note on flat mode (context)

The sweep also ran the flat index (`uniqsketch --sensitive -k81`, c=100) for
comparison. Flat is more sensitive at 0.01 % (32/56 detected) because of the larger
per-reference signature set, but it carried a much heavier false-positive load
(~1.0 spurious call per sample, max 3.08 % — a systematic Ec112↔Ec253 cross-map)
that also inflated its main-abundance error.

*Later correction:* the 3.08 % cross-map was not a property of flat indexing. It
was the `BloomFilter::insert_make_change` data race (fixed in 2.0, see CHANGELOG),
which let a k-mer shared by Ec112 and Ec253 become a signature for both; after the
fix the flat index's largest false call fell to 0.07 %. The residual sub-0.1 %
false calls of both layouts are the margin-1 error-read mechanism described at the
top of this document, and are what the 2.0 `--tree` margin default removes.
