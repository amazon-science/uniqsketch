# TreeUniqSketch (v2.0) accuracy validation

**Status:** Validation record for the v2.0 release (tree index + hierarchical
query). Benchmarked on simulated binary mixtures. The first part (September 2026)
records the study that set the 2.0 tree defaults; the sections after it are the
earlier reproduction check against the original TreeUniqSketch, kept for the
record and annotated where later work changed the interpretation.

## 2.0 tree defaults: what the read-origin oracle showed

The simulated reads carry their source genome in the read id
(`@AWS_Ec711_0_0/1`), so every signature hit can be classified as **true** (the
read came from the reference it hit) or **noise** (it came from another genome and
matched through sequencing errors). Everything below uses that split; the earlier
sweeps counted both as evidence.

### Setting

Same 8 phages and 280 mixtures as the sections below. The hard case is a
close-family member (one of Ec112/Ec377/Ec688/Ec711) at 0.01 %: 28 mixtures.
0.01 % of 3 M read pairs is ~300 reads, 0.27× coverage, and a close member's
distinguishing sequence is ~0.6 % of its genome (151–447 unique 81-mers in 2–3
loci). Per-base error rate of the reads, measured by 81-mer survival against the
source genome: 0.26 % (R1) / 0.41 % (R2). The dominant strain sits at ~2700×.

### Information ceiling

Taking each contaminant's *entire* unique-k-mer pool (no selection, no
thresholds), only **31 contaminant reads across all 28 mixtures** carry an intact
signature k-mer. 17/28 mixtures have ≥ 1 such read; **4/28 have ≥ 3**. With
`--rcutoff=2` (more than 2 reads) no signature scheme can exceed 4/28. Under the
margin-2 pool the ceiling is 25 reads, 16/28 mixtures with ≥ 1, 3/28 with ≥ 3.

### Noise

A margin-1 signature one substitution from an abundant sibling's k-mer is hit by
every sibling read with a sequencing error at that base, and one such read hits
up to 71 consecutive signatures, exactly like a true read. Over the 28 mixtures:

| index | true reads on contaminant | noise reads on contaminant | reads on absent references | strain calls at `r>2, h≥10` (TP / FP) | at `r≥1, h≥10` (TP / FP) |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `-c50`, margin 1 (previous default) | 18 | 23 | 71 | 8 / 4 | 10 / 4 |
| `-c500`, margin 1 | 31 | 60 | 2093 | 12 / 42 | 18 / 44 |
| `-c500 --min-margin=2 --strict-margin` | 25 | 1 | 2 | 3 / 0 | **16 / 0** |
| `-c50 --min-margin=2 --strict-margin` | 25 | 1 | 1 | 3 / 0 | 10 / 0 |

Of the 8 previous-default detections, 7 had a true read but were carried over the
threshold by noise, 1 was pure noise. Margin-2 strict removes the noise (a false
hit needs two coincident errors) while keeping 25 of 31 true reads; the close
members retain 62–99 % of their pools (Ec688 447 → 279 is the worst). With noise
gone, a single true read yields 24–71 hits (it covers a divergent locus) and the
three residual noise reads yield 1–5, so a single-read rule recovers every mixture
that has evidence. `-c50` reaches fewer because its sparser signatures give a read
fewer hits; `-c500` takes each close member's whole pool.

### Resulting 2.0 defaults

1. `uniqsketch --tree` selects with `--min-margin=2 --strict-margin` (opt out with
   `--min-margin=1`); parameters recorded in `<outdir>/build_params.tsv`.
2. `querysketch --tree` reads that record and defaults `--rcutoff` to 0 on a
   strict-margin index (explicit `--rcutoff` wins; older indexes keep 2).
3. Abundance is hits per signature (`count / sigs_total`), renormalised over the
   reported rows. With `-c500` the references carry 119–500 signatures and raw
   counts misreport a 10 % contaminant as anything from 2.6 % to 32 %; hits per
   signature gives 9.3–11.3 %. Equal signature counts make the two identical.

### Margin-2 cost on a bacterial set

120 complete *E. coli* RefSeq genomes, k=81, `-c1000`: every reference kept at
least 244 strict-margin signatures (mean 75 % of the margin-1 count); the closest
pair (pool 1036) kept 570. No reference fell below 50.

### Within-group leaf signatures (September 2026)

**Why.** A leaf's global pool (k-mers in no other genome of the index) is small
for a strain with close relatives, but mostly not because of the relatives: on the
120 *E. coli* genomes, the k-mers of a strain absent from its siblings but present
in some unrelated genome outnumbered its globally unique k-mers by a median 3.2×,
and by 26× for ASM4073563v1 (global pool 3,295 k-mers; within its 2-member group,
86,475). Those k-mers separate the strain from its siblings whenever their carriers
are absent from the sample, which for any given sample is almost all of them.

**What is built.** For every internal node with direct leaf children, candidates
unique among the node's descendant leaves are selected for its direct leaves with
the same margin and filters as ordinary signatures, and each signature's carriers
(other genomes of the index containing it) are recorded in
`<outdir>/sketch_group.tsv`. Signatures already in the leaf sketch are not
repeated. At query time a within-group signature is scored only if none of its
carriers is a candidate leaf after the coarse levels; global signatures are always
scored. Build: `uniqsketch --tree` default, `--no-group-signatures` to opt out
(recorded in `build_params.tsv`); query: `--no-group-signatures` ignores the file.

**Index.** E. coli (`--tree=1000000,300000,100000,10000 -c5000 -k81`): 27 groups,
20.95 M within-group candidates, 226,458 selected of which 26,307 were already
global signatures; 200,151 written over 76 leaves, 143,947 with at least one
carrier. Benchmark strains, global + within-group: ASM4073563v1 1255 + 2083,
ASM130812v1 592 + 245, ASM5700863v1 1253 + 429, ASM2155987v1 3018 + 2490,
ASM2052682v2 1363 + 323. Phage index: 23 signatures added.

**280-mixture sweep (E. coli), same scoring as above.**

| + within-group signatures | 39 | 11 | 11 | 0 | 0.006 → 0.317 |

**Sibling-pair benchmark (36 mixtures).** Pairs from the same group at 0.01, 0.03

|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| global only | 18 | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| within-group | 19 | 4 | 0 | 0 |

**Cost.** Build: the group build runs as flat parallel job lists (see

Query: 3.3 s vs 3.4 s on one 3 M-pair mixture at 32 threads with the single-pass
query engine (25.6 s vs 25.3 s before it; an earlier 578 s vs 305 s sweep
comparison was a cold-cache artefact, the "on" sweep having run first on freshly
simulated reads).

### Blocked Bloom filter (October 2026)

**What.** `BloomFilter` now names a cache-line-blocked filter
(`vendor/BlockedBloomFilter.hpp`): the leading hash selects one 512-bit block by
multiply-high (`(h0 · nBlocks) >> 64`, unbiased, no division, exact sizing) and
the remaining hashes select bits inside it, so an insert or a hit touches one
cache line instead of one per hash. The standard filter is kept as
`StandardBloomFilter`; `-DUNIQSKETCH_STANDARD_BF` restores it as the default.
The build records which layout built an index in `build_params.tsv`
(`bloom_filter`), and `querysketch --tree` warns when its own layout differs,
because the two draw different signatures from the same candidate pool.

**Why the gain is bounded.** At the 4 % fill of a `-b 128` filter the standard
`contains()` exits on the first unset bit, so a *miss* already costs about one
line (1.04 expected). Only hits and inserts touch all five. On a bacterial set
89 % of k-mer sightings in `loadBFfa` are repeats (hit all five lines, then set
five more in the solid filter), and that phase is ~41 % of the build; the
miss-dominated phases (`checkRef`, the 243 margin probes per candidate) gain
nothing. Predicted ceiling ~1.48×.

| set | threads | standard | blocked | | RSS |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|---|
| 120 *E. coli* tree | 32 | 150.7 s | 102.6 s | **1.46×** | 6115 → 6174 MB |
| 12 *E. coli* tree | 1 | 82.1 s | 52.9 s | 1.55× | unchanged |
| 12 *E. coli* tree | 32 | 32.3 s | 20.3 s | 1.59× | unchanged |
| 8 phages | 32 | 1.28 s | 1.28 s | 1.00× | filters fit in L2 |
| `comparesketch`, 120 × 120 | 32 | 62.9 s | 49.1 s | 1.28× | unchanged |
| query (plain / `--solid`) | 16 | 2.7 / 6.8 s | 2.8 / 6.8 s | 1.0× | — |

Microbenchmark (`test/bfbench.cpp`, 2^24 elements, equal memory): insert 1.5×,
probe-hit 1.3×, probe-miss unchanged, recall 100 %; FPR 4.8e-7 against 1.8e-7
at `-b 128`, 1.06× worse at `comparesketch`'s `-b 16 -d 3`.

**Effect on indexes.** A false positive in the distinct or solid filter drops a
candidate; on the 120-genome build 11 of 25.9 M candidates were lost, after
which selection redrew about 2 % of leaf signatures from the near-identical
pools (per-reference counts move by 1–4 on thousands, both directions). Phage
and 12-genome leaf pools identical; phage index byte-identical.

**Accuracy.** Both 280-mixture sweeps, blocked index and binary against the
standard-filter indexes from `4125419`, same scorer, run twice (experiment
binary, then the committed `bin/`): **every detection count, family call, false
row and MAE identical** at every abundance level; max-abs-error moves by
0.001 pp at two levels. Row-level, phage 280/280 byte-identical; *E. coli* 110
byte-identical, 169 differ in `count` only, 1 reports an unresolved 0.01 %
contaminant as its 4-member L2 group rather than its 20-member L0 group (both
correct ancestors; the same mixture flipped in the race-fix rebuild).

**`comparesketch`.** The 120 × 120 similarity table is not byte-identical: the
1.06× higher FPR at `-b 16` marks ~3e-4 more of each genome's k-mers as shared,
so 14,279 of 14,400 pairs report fewer unique k-mers (median relative change
3.7e-4, maximum 2.5e-3; similarity column shifts by at most 4.6e-4). Across the
`--cluster`/`--tree` thresholds used in this project (2 000 to 1 000 000), no
decision flips except one pair at 1,000,553 unique k-mers against the 1,000,000
threshold, which moves to 999,949. A pair 0.055 % from a threshold is a coin
flip under any thin filter (`-b 17` would move it too); this is a property of
`-b 16`, not of the layout. `comparesketch --fpr` or `--auto` sizes the filter
away from it.

### Partitioned parallel group scan (October 2026)

**The problem.** `identifyGroupUniqKmers` scanned a tree level's groups one per
thread, so a level could take no less than the serial scan of its largest
group. Timed phase by phase at 32 threads on the 120-genome *E. coli* build
(`scripts/profile_levels.sh`), the top level's filter phase took 12.2 s, of
which 11.1 s was the 20-genome cluster on one thread while 31 threads finished
the other 58 groups in about 3 s; the 12-genome MS8345 set (`dylan_repro`,
`--tree=3000000,1500000`), whose two coarse levels are single groups of 12 and
11 genomes, did not get faster past 16 threads at all (21.3 s at 32 threads,
55.3 s at one).

**What was done.** A group above an even share of the level's genomes
(`members / threads`) is now scanned by every thread (`loadBFfaGroupParallel`),
one such group at a time, before the remaining groups go one per thread, largest
first. The split is by *hash*, not by file: every filter is blocked, a k-mer
touches one 512-bit block per filter chosen by its leading hash `h0`, and block
counts are now rounded to a multiple of 256 (`BloomFilter::PARTITIONS`, at most
16 KB per filter), so the k-mers whose `h0` falls in the *p*-th 1/256 of the
hash space own the *p*-th 1/256 of the blocks of all four filters (group-private
`seen` and `repSeen`, shared distinct and solid). Contigs are read in file order
and hashed in parallel pieces into per-partition buckets; then one thread per
partition replays its k-mers in file order, deriving the other hashes from `h0`
as `ntHashIterator` does. Every block therefore sees exactly the sequence of
operations the serial scan performs on it, and the thread owning it is the only
writer, so the filters come out bit for bit the same as from the serial scan and
the stripe locks are skipped (`insert_make_change_exclusive`). Splitting by file
would have been simpler but not equivalent: which k-mers a Bloom filter
mistakes for present depends on which bits were set before them, and files
scanned concurrently have no fixed order. The standard filter spreads a k-mer's
bits over the whole array, so with `-DUNIQSKETCH_STANDARD_BF` the serial scan is
kept. Alongside: filters are zeroed in parallel (a gigabyte-scale memset on one
thread per level and per group-private filter; leaving pages to the kernel's
first-touch zeroing instead was slower, since the faults then land in the hot
loop), `-t` is applied before the tree is built rather than as a side effect of
the cardinality estimate, and each level reports its candidate and selection
time.

**Determinism, measured.** `scripts/verify_parallel_levels.sh`: on the MS8345
set the two coarse levels' candidate files and sketches are byte-identical
between `-t1` and `-t16`, `-t32`, `-t48`, `-t96`. The leaf level and the
within-group sketch differ between `-t1` and the others (4104 leaf k-mers, 154
group k-mers only in one side), which is the pre-existing behaviour of the
one-group-per-thread scans and not new: `scripts/repro_runs.sh` built the
*E. coli* index three times each with this code and with 94e4117, and in each
set one run of three differed from the other two in a single reference's
candidate count, by one k-mer (94e4117: one level-0 reference, 1117 sketch
k-mers and the group sketch; this code: one level-1 reference, 2402 k-mers).
The mechanism is not a race on the bits (those are atomic) but on the
*decision*: whether `insert_make_change` finds a k-mer "already present" depends
on which other k-mers' bits were set before it, so the false positives of the
shared distinct and solid filters depend on the order in which the concurrent
group scans interleave. The amplification is in selection: the slot width is
`total / needed`, so a candidate count one off shifts every slot boundary and
about half of that reference's signatures are redrawn from the same pool. On
the *E. coli* set, where every level mixes wide groups (now scanned
deterministically) with 56–119 narrow ones (still one per thread), `-t1`
against `-t32` differs in 5, 5, 2 and 3 references at levels 0–3 and in the
group sketch; the leaf sketch happened to match. The earlier note that a
sequential build of `idx_wg_c5000` was byte-identical to the parallel one was a
run that happened to match. Running every group, narrow ones included, through
the partitioned scan in a fixed order, so that any thread count reproduces the
serial result, is the next section.

**Speed.** *E. coli* 120-genome tree build at 32 threads, three runs each:
105.5–106.4 s → 86.8–87.7 s (1.22×), peak RSS 6.0 GB → 4.4 GB. Per level the
candidate phase (filters plus `checkRef`) went 13.2 → 5.9 s (level 0, three
groups of 20, 13 and 9 genomes scanned by all threads in 0.84 s), 9.2 → 5.8 s,
8.1 → 6.0 s, 6.8 → 6.1 s; what remains is the one-per-thread scan of the small
groups at the leaf level's memory-bound floor (~4 s for 120 genomes) and
`checkRef`. Selection is unchanged at ~6 s per level, 34 % of the build, and is
the next lever. MS8345 set: 21.3 → 9.3 s at 32 threads (2.3×; 9.4 s at 16, 9.6 s
at 96), 55.3 → 54.9 s at one thread (the serial path is unchanged).

**Effect on the index.** Rounding block counts moves every filter's
hash-to-block map, so a different handful of candidates fall to false positives
and selection redraws from the same pools: against 94e4117 on the *E. coli*
build, 13–17 references per level differ in candidate count (of 59–119) and
about 10 % of each level's sketch lines change, as the blocked filter itself
did. Sweeps: see below.

**Sweeps.** `scripts/run_sweeps_par.sh`, against the 4491e5e indexes and
sweeps. Phage: index byte-identical (its filters are small enough that the
rounding changed no decision), 280/280 result files byte-identical. *E. coli*:
detection table identical (0.01 %: 39/56 strain, 11 close, 11 family, 0 false

### Thread-count independence (October 2026)

**What.** Every scan of genomes into a distinct/solid filter pair now goes
through the partitioned scan of the previous section, generalized into
`GroupScanner`: the leaf level (each reference a group of one), each tree level
(its groups in partition order) and each within-group filter pair (the group's
members, one group at a time, into that group's own filters). Groups are
processed in list order and files within a group in order, so the result is bit
for bit what the serial scan produces, on any thread count. The one other
order-dependent step in the build went with it: `getUniqSet` claimed each
selected signature in a Bloom filter shared across the references of a sketch
(and the within-group build kept one such filter per group). A candidate is a
k-mer the solid filter does not contain, and a Bloom filter has no false
negatives, so a k-mer present in two references, or twice in one, is a candidate
for neither and the claim could never legitimately refuse; it could only drop a
valid candidate on a false positive (expected 0.03 per 120-genome build, from
the fill of a 128-bits-per-signature filter), and which candidate depended on
the order the threads reached it. It is gone. With the standard filter
(`-DUNIQSKETCH_STANDARD_BF`) there are no partitions and the groups are still
scanned one per thread; the guarantee is for the default filter.

file under the output directory, the leaf sketch and the stat files with their
directory stripped):

positives in the leaf and small-group scans) the serial order moves a different
handful of k-mers into the solid filters: 2, 4, 2 and 3 references at levels 0–3
and 3 within-group leaves differ by one candidate each (±1), which redraws 2.7,
2.6, 0.8 and 1.6 % of the level sketches and 2.7 % of the group sketch from the
same pools; the leaf sketch happened to match. Sweeps: see below.

**Sweeps** (`scripts/run_sweeps_det.sh`, reads from the NVMe copy, against the
6a12126 sweeps). Phage: index byte-identical, 280/280 result files
byte-identical, 506 s for the sweep (2555 s from EBS). *E. coli*: index as

**What remains order-dependent.** Nothing in the index. In the query,
`--solid`'s read filter swallows the first sighting of each k-mer, and which
read that is depends on the order the batches are processed, so the
distinct-reads column can move by a few between runs (the count column does
not); and the `logread_*` read-name lists come out of an `unordered_set`, in
processing order. Both are documented in the query section above.

### Query: one parallel region, and the gzip floor (October 2026)

**What.** `queryReads` ran one OpenMP parallel region per 4096-read batch; the
main thread took the batch from the reader queue and merged alone, and the
other threads spun at the barrier until the next batch. The readers (one per
file, decompress + parse) deliver a batch every few milliseconds and the team
hashes it in a fraction of that, so the spin was most of the process. Now the
team is one region for the whole sample: each thread takes a batch, hashes it,
merges its hits under a lock, and sleeps on the queue's condition variable when
it is empty. The result does not depend on merge order (counts, and per-target
*sets* of read names); with `--solid`, which read's sighting the filter swallows
depends on processing order, so the distinct-reads column can move by a few,
as it did between runs before (the old binary itself gave 4478 and 4479 on the
same phage sample in different runs).

**Measured** (`scripts/gzip_bench.sh`, one 3 M-pair *E. coli* mixture, 2 × 900
MB FASTQ, gzip -6 = 181 + 199 MB, 120-genome tree index, page cache warm):

| gzip | 94e4117 | 32 | 5.63 s | 108.3 s |
| gzip | 94e4117, `OMP_WAIT_POLICY=passive` | 32 | 5.82 s | 31.7 s |
| gzip | this | 32 | 5.41 s | 25.5 s |
| gzip | this + zlib-ng (`LD_PRELOAD`) | 32 | 3.99 s | 22.4 s |
| gzip | this | 4 | 6.07 s | 23.7 s |

input to the plain-FASTQ floor and plain input below it, is to take parsing off
the decompression thread (decompress into large chunks on one thread, parse on
another, or parse chunks in parallel at record boundaries). Not done here.

The family machinery does its job (contaminant found at strain or family level
in 41, 48, 52, 55, 56 of 56); strain resolution collapses and false strain rows
appear, 81 in 70 of the 280 mixtures, with up to 31 442 hits and 5 667 distinct
reads behind one of them. The probes on the smaller indexes show the onset: at
1 000 genomes the 0.01 % Esc21complete contaminant, resolved to strain by the
120-genome index, is reported as its 81-member level-0 family; at 2 000 genomes
the 90 % main of the first probe (SEC470) is absent from the output altogether.
Three mechanisms, each measured:

1. *Pruning on signatures the member does not carry.* A node's signatures are
   k-mers of its representative absent from every genome outside the node. In a
   dense set the representative's node-unique k-mers sit in its private
   accessory sequence, which the other members do not share, so a member's reads
   hit none of them; the query prunes the member at that level (one hit and one
   read is the floor) and its own signatures are never consulted. Measured for
   every (leaf, ancestor) pair (`scripts/ancestor_presence_all.py`): the median
   share of a node's signatures present in a member genome is 73 % at 120
   genomes and 13 % at 5 493; for nodes of more than 100 leaves it is 0.3 %, and
   49 % of those members carry none at all. Leaves with at least one ancestor
   whose signatures they carry none of: 0 of 120, 42 of 500 (8 %), 188 of
   1 000 (19 %), 483 of 2 000 (24 %), 2 408 of 5 493 (44 %). SEC470 at 2 000
   genomes: 0 of its 27-leaf node's 3 023 signatures are in its genome, so it is
   pruned at level 0 with 1 752 leaf signatures and 1 939 within-group
   signatures unused, at 90 % abundance.
2. *Signature pools vanish.* Genomes with no 81-mer unique against the rest of
   the index: 1 of 120, 9 of 500, 13 of 1 000, 42 of 2 000, 225 of 5 493;
   absent from the leaf sketch after the margin and entropy filters: 1, 13, 27,
   73, 416; under 100 signatures: 0, 7, 25, 50, 210. Of the 8 benchmark genomes,
   which had 1 063–4 056 leaf signatures in the 120-genome index, six have 0,
   0, 45, 72, 74 and 77 at 5 493 (SEC470 is contained in other K-12
   derivatives; the Esc* isolates have many close relatives in RefSeq). These
   are not resolvable at strain level by any choice of signatures; the family
   level and the within-group signatures are what the design offers them, and
   this is where the third mechanism bites.
3. *The carrier mask is blind to carriers that are not candidates.* A
   within-group signature is admitted at the leaf level unless one of its
   carriers is a candidate leaf (`scored`). A carrier that was pruned by (1), or
   that has no global signatures and so never appears in the leaf sketch's
   reference list (2), is not scored, so its signatures in its relatives are
   admitted and its reads hit them. Of the 223 003 hits behind the 81 false
   strain rows, 222 802 are on within-group signatures whose recorded carriers
   include the sample's main genome (2 242 signatures) or contaminant (396);
   the remaining 201 are global-signature hits of the single-read noise class
   (two rows, 10 and 12 hits, one read each). The first probe at 5 493 is the
   type case: SEC470 has no signatures of any kind, is carried forward as a
   candidate by the "no representative scored it" rule, yet is never `scored`,
   so GCF_002951735's 148 within-group signatures that SEC470 carries are
   admitted and collect 5 667 of SEC470's reads.

The build is correct throughout: none of the 2 199 leaf signatures of
GCF_002951735 occurs in SEC470's genome; the false hits are all on within-group
signatures, which are unique within their group by definition and carry their
carriers for exactly this purpose.

**What this means.** The signature definition and the build scale; the
hierarchical query's two pruning assumptions do not: that a member carries its
ancestors' signatures, and that a genome present in the sample is always a
candidate leaf. The next section is the fix. The three super-linear build
steps and the text sketch load are separate items, not done.

**Memory: the `seen` filter bound (October 2026).** A group's seen filter can

### Query at scale: own evidence, carriers, breadth; and spread selection (October 2026)

Five changes, made and validated together because the gut-metagenome negative
control connects them: the first two fix the scaling failures above, the third
is what the negative control then demanded, the fourth is what the negative
control showed about the sketch, and the fifth is what the 5 493-genome sweep
still showed after the first four.

**1. A leaf is in play on its own evidence.** At the leaf level a leaf stays in
play if the narrowing kept it, *or* its own globally unique signatures clear the
reporting thresholds (`--hit`, `--rcutoff`), *or* its within-group signatures
that no other genome carries do. Two floors were tried first and rejected on
the 120-genome sweep: the pruning floor (one hit, one read) let 268 of 300
"rescued" leaves in on one read on one signature, the single-error noise the
strict margin keeps below threshold, and as carriers (see 2) they withheld up
to 7 % of a present genome's hits; and counting hits on *carried* within-group
signatures as a leaf's own evidence put a 0.01 % contaminant's reads on the
within-group signatures of eight unrelated leaves it carried and reported them
as strains (49 false rows), because such a hit is evidence that the leaf *or a
carrier* is present.

**2. Carriers are recognised by name, from any level.** `uniqsketch --tree`
writes `<outdir>/leaves.tsv` (refId, name, path), so a leaf with no signatures
can still be named. The within-group mask withholds a signature if any carrier
*may be present*: in play by 1, or, for a leaf with no global signatures, covered
at any coarse level by a representative with evidence, whether or not the
narrowing later dropped it. Only the signature-less leaf gets the lineage rule:
a leaf that has global signatures and is present hits them (they are spread
along its genome, see 4), so its absence from play means absence. Applying the
lineage rule to every leaf (tried first) made all 20 members of a present
main's level-0 node block, and cost a 0.01 % sibling 175–388 of its
within-group signatures. The case that forced this: on the 5 493-genome index a
99.99 % main (Esc13) has no global signatures and is pruned at level 1; it
carries verbatim 10 of the 0.01 % contaminant's within-group signatures, and
976 of its reads were credited to the contaminant (count 994 for a true 0.01 %
presence whose own 18 signatures were hit once each). With the mask: count 19,
19 signatures, 2 reads, abundance 0.00013 against a truth of 0.0001.

**3. A strain row must be broad.** With λ = count/sigs_total hits per
signature, a present genome hits about sigs_total·(1−e^−λ) distinct signatures;
one locus of it, in some other organism, piles the same count onto the few
signatures there. `--tree-breadth=F` (default 0.5) withholds a strain row whose
sigs_hit is below F times that expectation (`breadthRatio`). The ratio over
every true strain row of the four sweeps, before and after 4:

| sweep | true strain rows | ratio min / 1 % / median | false rows (all single-read noise) |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 120 *E. coli* | 543 | 0.76 / 0.91 / 1.00 | 0 |
| 8 phages | 548 | 0.73 / 0.92 / 1.00 | 2, ratio 1.04 |
| 1000 *E. coli* | 524 | 0.73 / 0.88 / 1.00 | 4, ratio 1.00–1.01 |
| 5 493 *E. coli* (after 2) | 363 | 0.67 / 0.99 / 1.00 | ratio 1.00–1.03 |

The gut call (below) scores 0.035–0.058; the family-level erm(B) call that began
the repeat investigation would have scored 0.02. Read-limited calls are not
affected (λ ≪ 1: the expectation is the count itself, and distinct reads hit
distinct signatures). The floor is *not* applied to family rows: a node's
signatures are its representative's, a present member carries only part of
them (36 % for one node; median 13 % across the 5 493-genome tree), and the
first version that applied it withheld 42 correct family rows.

**The negative control, and what it showed about the sketch.** With 1 and 2 in
place the 120-genome index reported `GCF_008124265` (O16:H48) on SRR061226, a
human gut metagenome with no *Escherichia*: 4 687 hits, 717 reads, 155
signatures, all 155 inside one 785-bp window at 414 399–415 183 of its
chromosome, a 46 %-GC insertion with *E. coli* core flanks that gut bacteria
carry. The old query had never reported it only because it pruned that leaf at
level 0 for the reason 1 fixes. The sketch had put 21 % of that genome's
chromosomal signatures into 3 % of its candidates (191 per kb against 0.15
elsewhere), because selection spaced its slots along the *candidate list* and
fill rates differ by locus: in the core, most candidates are one substitution
from a relative and fail the strict margin; in a foreign insertion every
position is a candidate and every one passes. Across the 120 references, 20 had
a tenth or more of their signatures in one 2-kb window; across 5 077 on the
5 493-genome index, 1 804 had a fifth or more and 624 had everything in one
window (`scripts/signature_clumping.py`).

**4. Selection along the genome.** The genome is cut into `quota` windows of
equal length; the first usable candidate of each is taken; the unfilled quota
goes to the windows that supplied, in proportion to their candidates, capped at
a common level chosen so the shares add up to the quota, each share laid out as
equal runs of the window's candidate list. The cap binds only when the supplied
windows' shares exceed the quota, that is for a reference that reaches it, which
then gets a flat layout; below the quota the allocation is the old one with the
slots the old grid left empty now filled. Two rules that flattened regardless of
the quota failed on the 120-genome sweep: an equal share per supplied window gave
a window whose supply is one 74-bp cluster the same dozen picks as a window with
candidates throughout, 12–17 signatures inside one read length, and a relative's
read carrying two coincident errors hit all of them (4 false single-read rows);
and taking the share as the next usable candidates put it at consecutive
positions (18 such rows). What the rule does on the validation indexes
(`scripts/clump_compare.py`, `scripts/sig_overlap.py`):

| index | at quota | signatures | densest 2-kb window share, median / refs ≥ 10 % | old signatures kept |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| 120 *E. coli*, `-c5000` | 0 of 119 | 352 797 → 373 001 | 0.027 / 20 → 0.026 / 20 | 24 % (7 refs unchanged) |
| 8 phages, `-c500` | 3 of 8 | 2 835 → 2 841 | — | 35 % (the 4 below quota that take their whole pool unchanged; the other 4 keep 1–101 of 500) |
| 5 493 *E. coli*, `-c5000` | 1 of 5 077 | — | — | — |

So the signatures move but their concentration does not: the 785-bp window keeps
its 150 signatures (that genome has 2-safe candidates in only 63 of 5 000
windows, so any rule that honours the quota gives each supplied window dozens),
and the breadth floor is what sees that 62 of those windows were silent.
Detection and abundance tables are unchanged on both sweeps, row for row.

**5. One read counts only for a leaf whose lineage is attested.** With 1–4 in
place the 5 493-genome sweep still had 43 false strain rows, every one a single
read of 10–38 hits, 35 of them leaves joining by own evidence and 39 with no
ancestor in common with the main (`scripts/classify_false_rows.py`): a read of
the dominant genome carrying two coincident errors reproduces a run of
consecutive signature k-mers of an unrelated genome, which across thousands of
close genomes happens often enough to show. Every true single-read row, by
contrast, belonged to a family whose node had 48–52 858 reads. Requiring two
reads of every leaf joining by own evidence (tried first, `sweep_r2`) removed
the 35 but cost resolution, because the rescue is judged on the leaf's global
signatures and the within-group ones nobody else carries, not on all it is
scored with: in the 1 000-genome index Esc24's 762 global signatures all lie on
a 65-kb plasmid that gets 1.2 % of its reads, and at 0.1 % one of those 40
reads had a clean 81-mer while 29 reads hit its 1 580 carried within-group
signatures; in the 5 493-genome index the four Esc strains keep 74–146
signatures at one or two loci each, so even at 3 % a single read is all that
hits them. Gating the single read on the mask's `everInPlay` instead (tried
second, `sweep_r3`: one hit on any representative covering the leaf) removed 2
of the 43: a stray hit on an ancestor's thousands of signatures is as common as
the stray run on the leaf's own, and nearly every lineage has one. The rule is
therefore: a leaf joins at `--rcutoff` if some representative covering it
cleared `--hit` on two or more reads at some level (`lineageAttested`), and
needs two reads of its own otherwise.

### Flat breadth floor and `--circular` (October 2026)

Four changes came out of comparing 5548d8c with the public 1.6.1 on three internal
phage QC analyses (40 vector references, real reads): the tree query's breadth
test made available to flat queries (`--breadth`), `uniqsketch --circular`, a
`members` column on family rows, and a warning for references whose candidate
pool the filters consume entirely. Their own evidence is the three QC cases;
this section is what the two validation sets add.

**Nothing moves at default flags.** The phage and 120-genome indexes rebuilt
by the patched `uniqsketch` are byte-identical to the 5548d8c indexes apart from
the new `circular 0` line in `build_params.tsv`; the tree sweeps of both are
identical to `sweep_r4` row for row, count for count (554 and 557 rows), the
negative control reports nothing on either, and `make test` and `ctest` pass
with both filter layouts. The one visible change is the appended `members`
column (`-` on strain rows), which the scoring scripts, parsing by header, did
not notice.

**Flat `--breadth=0.5` costs nothing on the sweeps and closes the real false
positive.** Both sets were queried flat (no `--tree`, so `--rcutoff` 2) with and
without the floor:

| set | flat, contaminant strain rows at 0.01 / 0.1 / 1 / 3 / 10 % (of 56) | false rows | with `--breadth=0.5` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 120 *E. coli* | 27 / 50 / 56 / 56 / 56 (close relatives 0 / 22 / 28 / 28 / 28) | 0 | 0 rows withheld; 280 outputs byte-identical |
| 8 phages | 31 / 53 / 56 / 56 / 56 (close relatives 3 / 25 / 28 / 28 / 28) | 0 | 0 rows withheld; 280 outputs byte-identical |

(The flat numbers are the tree's missing its family rows and single-read calls:
the tree reports 39 and 44 of 56 at 0.01 %.) On SRR061226 the flat query of the
120-genome index reports `GCF_008124265.1` at abundance 1 with 3 592 hits from
709 reads on 119 of its 2 613 signatures, the 785-bp insertion the tree query
withholds at breadth 0.058; `--breadth=0.5` withholds it at 0.061 and the table
is empty, as it already is for the phage index. On that evidence the flat
default is 0.5, the tree's. The one legitimate casualty is a sample whose reads
cover one locus by design (an amplicon); `--breadth=0` is the old behaviour.

**`--circular` on the validation sets.** The 8 phages are single contigs: four
gain 29–80 candidates, the four near-identical ones none, and the leaf sketch is
byte-identical, so the sweep is too. The 120 complete *E. coli* genomes (1–9
circular contigs each) gain 7 018 candidates net (70 references gain, 12 lose,
38 unchanged); 96.7 % of the leaf k-mers are kept, 576 of 373 184 leaf
signatures are wrap k-mers (start position above L−k, on 55 references; a
small-pool genome takes all of its), and the index is byte-identical at `-t16`
and `-t32`. The tree sweep keeps every row of every mixture: same detections,
same family rows, no false row, abundance moving at most 0.0034 (contaminant
MAE 0.006 / 0.021 / 0.078 / 0.141 / 0.308 against 0.006 / 0.020 / 0.081 / 0.144 /
0.312), negative control empty. The counts that move belong to the five
benchmark genomes, and the largest mover is the mechanism itself:
`Esc21complete` loses 78 of its 1 063 leaf signatures, one 78-bp run at
positions 629–706 of its plasmid 4, and all 78 are the wrap k-mers of
`Esc24complete`'s 9 373-bp plasmid, the same molecule at another origin. They
were `Esc21complete`'s signatures only because `Esc24complete`'s rendering
hides them. Simulated reads never span an origin, so in these sweeps they only
ever counted for `Esc21complete` itself (5 500–6 500 hits per mixture); with
real `Esc24complete` reads they would fire for `Esc21complete`, which the tree
breadth floor would withhold and flat mode before `--breadth` would not. The
sweeps therefore price `--circular` (no detection change, 0.15 % dead
signatures on linear reads) rather than show its benefit; that is the phage QC
case, reproduced here at signature level: without the pad the rotated rendering
of the self genome leaves PW0979 two signatures at its break locus (positions
10 422 and 10 497), with it none, and the two renderings converge on identical
candidate pools (12 824 for PW0979, 14 933 for the self genome), as a circular
genome's should.

The pad applies to every contig of every reference, which is right for complete
genomes and wrong for a draft assembly, whose contig ends are not joins; RefSeq
FASTA headers carry no topology, so this stays a flag. Scripts:
`scripts/handoff_sweeps.sh` (the chain), `scripts/withheld_report.py` (rows a
flat breadth floor withheld, by truth), `scripts/circ_index_report.py`
(candidate deltas, wrap signatures and overlap of a `--circular` index against
the default build), all outside the repo.

## TL;DR (July 2026 reproduction check)

- The v2.0 **tree** query reproduces the original TreeUniqSketch abundance
  estimates almost exactly: **Pearson r = 0.9989 (R² = 0.998)** on log10 detected
  abundance across 224 jointly-detected contaminants; mean absolute difference
  **0.08 percentage points**.
- Where the two differ, v2.0 is **mildly better** — slightly more sensitive at the
  detection floor and slightly more accurate on abundance — with a negligible
  increase in tiny (< 0.1 %) false calls. No regression.
- Main reference detected in **100 %** of samples at every abundance; contaminant
  detected in **100 %** at ≥ 1 %, ~98 % at 0.1 %; 0.01 % is at the coverage floor.

## Benchmark setup

**References.** 8 phage genomes (~170 kb each), `simulated/all_set/AWS_Ec{112,151,
226,253,377,647,688,711}.fasta`.

**Samples.** 280 paired-end mixtures, 3 M read pairs each
(`simulated/reads/reads_3000000_abund_<main>_<contam>_<pct>_R{1,2}.fastq`):
8 mains × 7 contaminants × 5 mixing ratios (contaminant at 0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 3.0,
10.0 %). Ground truth is the mixing ratio in the filename — expected main =
`100 − pct`, expected contaminant = `pct`.

**Index (public v2.0 binary).**

```bash
uniqsketch --tree=10000,1000,100 -c50 -t32 -k81 \
    -o tree/sketch_uniq.tsv -f tree/outdir -r tree/db_uniq_count.tsv @all_set.txt
```

The tree groups the 4 near-identical genomes {Ec112, Ec377, Ec688, Ec711} into one
cluster (internal node, representative Ec377) and keeps the 4 distinct genomes
{Ec151, Ec226, Ec253, Ec647} separate — 8 leaves, 1 internal node, level-1 sketch
of 5 representatives, full-resolution leaf sketch of all 8.

**Query.**

```bash
querysketch --sensitive -t32 --tree=tree/outdir --ref tree/sketch_uniq.tsv \
    --r1 R1.fastq --r2 R2.fastq --out out.tsv
```

The full sweep (all 280 × tree, plus flat for context) is `scripts/run_all_queries_v2.sh`.

## Tree results

Detection is scored per sample against the filename ratio; abundance error is the
mean absolute difference (percentage points) over detected contaminants.

**Contaminant (56 samples per abundance level):**

| expected % | detected / 56 | detection rate | mean abs err (pp) |
|-----------:|--------------:|---------------:|------------------:|
| 0.01 | 15 | 27 % | 0.018 |
| 0.1  | 55 | 98 % | 0.023 |
| 1.0  | 56 | 100 % | 0.079 |
| 3.0  | 56 | 100 % | 0.111 |
| 10.0 | 56 | 100 % | 0.214 |

**Main reference:** detected in all 280 samples (100 % at every level); mean
absolute abundance error ranges 0.015 pp (at 0.01 % contaminant) to 0.21 pp (at
10 %).

**False positives** (a detected reference that is neither main nor contaminant):
0.15 rows per sample, maximum magnitude 0.08 %. Effectively clean.

The 0.01 % row is the coverage floor: a 0.01 % contaminant is ~300 read pairs over
a 170 kb genome (~0.26× coverage), so most signature k-mers are simply not
sequenced. Detection at ≥ 0.1 % is essentially complete.

## Reproduction check: v2.0 tree vs. original TreeUniqSketch

The original results (`simulated/results_tree/summary.tsv`, produced by the old
main-repo binary querying the same c=50 tree leaf sketch) are the closest
apples-to-apples reference for the v2.0 tree path. Comparing contaminant detected
abundance over the same 280 samples:

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| Pearson r (log10 detected abundance, n = 224) | **0.9989** |
| R² | 0.998 |
| mean \|v2.0 − old\| detected abundance | 0.084 pp |
| detection agreement (of 280) | 264 (224 both-detect, 40 both-miss) |
| detections v2.0 gained (old missed) | 14 |
| detections v2.0 lost (old detected) | 2 |

**Which is better — v2.0 tree.** The two are near-identical (r = 0.9989), and where
they diverge v2.0 is slightly ahead:

- **Sensitivity:** v2.0 detects the contaminant in more samples at the floor
  (0.01 %: 15/56 vs 5/56; 0.1 %: 55/56 vs 53/56); net +12 detections.
- **Accuracy:** lower mean abs error at essentially every level, for both the
  contaminant and the main (e.g. main at 10 %: 0.21 vs 0.35 pp).
- **Cost:** v2.0 emits a few more sub-0.1 % false calls (0.15 vs 0.02 per sample) —
  the expected flip side of higher sensitivity — but no large spurious calls in
  either (max FP < 0.09 % for both).

So v2.0 reproduces the reference and nudges it forward: a small net improvement in
sensitivity and abundance accuracy, not a regression.

## Note on flat mode (context)

The sweep also ran the flat index (`uniqsketch --sensitive -k81`, c=100) for
comparison. Flat is more sensitive at 0.01 % (32/56 detected) because of the larger
per-reference signature set, but it carried a much heavier false-positive load
(~1.0 spurious call per sample, max 3.08 % — a systematic Ec112↔Ec253 cross-map)
that also inflated its main-abundance error.

*Later correction:* the 3.08 % cross-map was not a property of flat indexing. It
was the `BloomFilter::insert_make_change` data race (fixed in 2.0, see CHANGELOG),
which let a k-mer shared by Ec112 and Ec253 become a signature for both; after the
fix the flat index's largest false call fell to 0.07 %. The residual sub-0.1 %
false calls of both layouts are the margin-1 error-read mechanism described at the
top of this document, and are what the 2.0 `--tree` margin default removes.
